## The Digital Archive and the 1998 Certification

A Mexican certification dated **December 12, 1998** is signed by **Cardinal Norberto Rivera Carrera, Archbishop Primate of Mexico**.

The certificate states in Spanish:

> **“Certifico que la presente impresión es una reproducción fiel del archivo digital del Sagrado Original de la Imagen de Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe.”**

### English Translation

> **“I certify that this print is a faithful reproduction of the digital archive of the Sacred Original of the Image of Our Lady of Guadalupe.”**

At the bottom of the certificate appears the statement:

> **“Captura del archivo digital y reproducción: DIGITAL COLOR S.A. DE C.V.”**

### English Translation

> **“Digital archive capture and reproduction: DIGITAL COLOR S.A. DE C.V.”**

This documentation identifies **Digital Color, S.A. de C.V.** as responsible for the digital capture and reproduction and establishes that the digital archive existed no later than December 12, 1998.

Most importantly, Cardinal Rivera's certification describes the associated print as a **“reproducción fiel” — a faithful reproduction — of the digital archive of the Sacred Original.**

### What this certification means

The certificate provides contemporaneous documentation for three important points:

- The source was identified as the **digital archive of the Sacred Original Image of Our Lady of Guadalupe**.
- **Digital Color, S.A. de C.V.** was identified with the digital capture and reproduction.
- **Cardinal Norberto Rivera Carrera** certified the associated print as a **faithful reproduction** of that digital archive.

The certificate should not be interpreted as stating that Cardinal Rivera individually certified every later reproduction or every canvas printed today.

## The Virgen Peregrina and Pope Saint John Paul II

A second Mexican certification identifies the image as the **“Virgen Peregrina” — the Pilgrim Virgin**.

Its inscription states:

> **“Imagen de Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe conocida como ‘Virgen Peregrina’, bendecida por S.S. Juan Pablo II el 25 de enero de 1999 y que recorrió el territorio nacional de enero de 1999 a abril del 2000, con motivo del Gran Jubileo de los 2000 años de nuestra Redención.”**

### English Translation

> **“Image of Our Lady of Guadalupe known as the ‘Pilgrim Virgin,’ blessed by His Holiness John Paul II on January 25, 1999, which traveled throughout the national territory from January 1999 to April 2000 on the occasion of the Great Jubilee of the 2,000 years of our Redemption.”**

This certification documents two important events in the history of the image:

**January 25, 1999 — Papal Blessing**  
The image known as the *Virgen Peregrina* was blessed by **Pope John Paul II** during his apostolic visit to Mexico.

**January 1999 – April 2000 — National Pilgrimage**  
The *Virgen Peregrina* traveled throughout Mexico in connection with the **Great Jubilee of 2000**.

Pope John Paul II was canonized in 2014 and is now known as **Pope Saint John Paul II**.

## A Church-Connected Digital Reproduction Project

Catholic sources describing the Missionary Images of Our Lady of Guadalupe provide additional historical context for the digital reproduction project.

The **Roman Catholic Diocese of Orange** describes its Missionary Image as a Catholic Church-authorized digital replica of the original Guadalupe tilma and reports that a high-resolution digital image was created from the original tilma for the Missionary Image project.

Other Catholic accounts describe the project as having been undertaken with Church approval and associate Cardinal Norberto Rivera Carrera with authorization of the digitization project.

These later accounts provide useful historical context. The strongest direct evidence for the Primos Maternos provenance claim, however, remains the contemporaneous **December 12, 1998 certification itself**, which identifies the digital archive, Digital Color, S.A. de C.V., and Cardinal Rivera's certification of fidelity.

## Understanding the Provenance

The historical record can be understood as a chain:

### 1. The Sacred Original

The original image of Our Lady of Guadalupe is preserved at the **Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe in Mexico City**.

### 2. The Digital Archive

A digital archive was created from the Sacred Original. The December 12, 1998 certification identifies **Digital Color, S.A. de C.V.** with the digital capture and reproduction.

### 3. Certification of Fidelity

Cardinal **Norberto Rivera Carrera**, Archbishop Primate of Mexico, certified that the associated print was a **faithful reproduction of the digital archive of the Sacred Original**.

### 4. The Virgen Peregrina

The image known as the **Virgen Peregrina** was blessed by **Pope John Paul II on January 25, 1999**.

### 5. The Great Jubilee Journey

The certified inscription records that the image traveled throughout Mexico from **January 1999 through April 2000** in connection with the Great Jubilee.

### 6. Primos Maternos

Primos Maternos uses a digital image from this historical image lineage to produce its canvas reproductions.

**Our canvases are printed in the United States.**

The phrase **“faithful reproduction”** comes directly from the December 12, 1998 Mexican certification:
