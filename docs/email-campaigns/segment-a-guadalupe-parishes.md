## Email 3 — The image itself (value, not sales)

**Subject:** What the Aztecs read in the Guadalupe image

**Preview text:** Every fold, sash, and star was a message. A short read for your Guadalupe ministry or December 12 preparation.

**Body:**

Dear @firstname,

Not a sales email — just an article your Guadalupe ministry may find useful for catechesis, a bulletin insert, or December 12 preparation.

Spanish missionaries preached in Mexico for ten years before the tilma with almost no result. In the ten years after Juan Diego opened his cloak in front of the bishop, roughly nine million Aztecs were baptized. Historians of the image now understand why: to the Aztecs the image was a codex, written in symbols they read at a glance.

The mestiza face anticipating a race that didn't yet exist. Hair parted in the middle with no tie — an Aztec sign for a pregnant virgin. The black sash — the Aztec maternity belt. The four-petaled Nahui Ollin over the womb — the Aztec glyph for the presence of God. The stars on her mantle matching the actual sky over Mexico City on the morning of December 12, 1531.
