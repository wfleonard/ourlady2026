# Segment A — NEPQ Rewrite

Three-email sequence for parishes and shrines named for Our Lady of Guadalupe, rewritten in the NEPQ style (Jeremy Miner / *Black Book of Questions*).

Original pitch-driven version stays at [`segment-a-guadalupe-parishes.md`](segment-a-guadalupe-parishes.md) for A/B testing or a fallback.

## What's different from the original

| Traditional (current) | NEPQ version (this file) |
|---|---|
| Statements about the product | Questions about the prospect's situation |
| "We offer..." opens the email | A curious question opens the email |
| Product features named up-front | Product barely mentioned — reader asks about it |
| CTA is "Order for Your Parish →" | CTA is "worth a quick reply?" |
| Pitch drives the email | Prospect's answer drives the email |
| ~350 words each | ~180 words each — shorter, tighter |

NEPQ moves the seller from "here's why we're worth it" to "here's what I'm curious about — how do you think about this?" The email invites a reply, not a click. Replies are the currency.

## Cadence and personalization — unchanged

Same 10-day cadence, same Gmail multi-send merge fields (`@firstname`, `@parishname`, `@city`, `@state`, `@contactrole`, `@guadalupeconnection`). Same UTM tag on outbound links: `utm_campaign=parish-outreach-2026&utm_content=a-nepq-<n>`.

## Notes on the NEPQ voice

**Words that stay in.** *Curious. A pattern I've noticed. What would that look like? Would it be a bad idea? Worth a quick reply?* These are NEPQ's connective tissue — they signal the seller isn't defending anything, and they invite the prospect to think out loud.

**Words that stay out.** *We offer. Our product. Church-authorized. The only. Ready to hang. Free shipping.* These are declarative claims. NEPQ's whole point is that when the prospect sells themselves on the need, features stop mattering. Features re-enter only when the prospect asks.

**Escape hatches.** Every email ends with two exits — reply, or click. NEPQ prefers reply. The click is there for people who are ready to buy without a conversation, so we don't lose the transaction while waiting for a reply we might not get.

**Reply expectation.** Segment A is 20 parishes. Historical NEPQ reply rates on cold B2B email are 8–15% when the technique is applied well. That's 2–3 real conversations from this send, which is the point — you don't want 20 fast noes, you want 3 good ones.

**Length.** Each email is ~180 words versus ~350 in the original. NEPQ reads shorter because it's not defending a claim, it's asking a question. A long NEPQ email starts sounding like a survey.
